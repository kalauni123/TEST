Supplementary Information & Detailed Worked Calculations

1. Theoretical Foundation & Formula Derivation

When a small steel ball bearing of radius $r$ and density $\rho$ falls vertically under gravity through a viscous liquid (Glycerin) of density $\sigma$, three forces act on it:

$$\text{Downward Weight (Gravity): } W = m g = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho g$$ $$\text{Upward Buoyant Force (Archimedes): } U = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \sigma g$$ $$\text{Upward Viscous Drag Force (Stoke's Law): } F = 6\pi\eta r v$$

At equilibrium (terminal velocity $v$), upward forces equal downward force ($W = F + U$):

$$\frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \rho g = 6\pi\eta r v + \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \sigma g$$ $$6\pi\eta r v = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 (\rho - \sigma) g \implies \eta = \frac{2}{9} \frac{(\rho - \sigma) g r^2}{v}$$

2. Diameter Formula Transformation ($t \cdot D^2$)

Substituting terminal velocity $v = \frac{L}{t}$ and radius $r = \frac{D}{2}$ into Stoke's equation:

$$\eta = \frac{2}{9} \frac{(\rho - \sigma) g \left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^2}{\frac{L}{t}} = \frac{(\rho - \sigma) g}{18 L} t D^2$$

Since $\eta$ is constant at a fixed temperature, $t D^2 = \text{constant}$. Thus, a plot of $t$ vs $\frac{1}{D^2}$ is a straight line passing through origin!

NEWTON'S VISCOSITY LAW & STOKE'S LAW

According to Newton's Law of Viscosity, the tangential viscous force $F$ between fluid layers of area $A$ moving with velocity gradient $\frac{dv}{dx}$ is:

$$F = -\eta A \frac{dv}{dx}$$

According to Stoke's law, for a sphere of radius $r$ moving with velocity $v$:

$$F = 6\pi\eta r v$$
Viva Voce Questions & Answers
1. What is viscosity? ▼
Viscosity is the intrinsic property of a fluid by virtue of which it opposes relative motion between its consecutive layers.
2. State Stoke's law. ▼
Stoke's law states that when a spherical body of radius $r$ moves through a viscous medium of viscosity $\eta$ with terminal velocity $v$, the viscous drag force is $F = 6\pi\eta r v$.
3. Distinguish between streamline and turbulent motion of a liquid. ▼
In streamline flow, fluid velocity at any point remains constant in magnitude and direction over time. In turbulent flow, velocity exceeds critical velocity, creating chaotic motion and eddies.
4. Define terminal velocity. ▼
Terminal velocity is the maximum constant velocity acquired by a spherical body falling through a viscous fluid when downward weight is balanced by upward buoyant force and viscous drag.
5. Define coefficient of viscosity. ▼
The coefficient of viscosity ($\eta$) is defined as the tangential backward force per unit area of layer required to maintain unit velocity gradient normal to the direction of flow.
6. What is steady flow? ▼
Flow is steady when fluid parameters (velocity, pressure, density) at any given position remain invariant with time.
7. Find the dimension of $\eta$. ▼
Dimension = $[M L^{-1} T^{-1}]$.
8. What are units of coefficient of viscosity in CGS and SI? ▼
In CGS: Poise ($\text{g}\cdot\text{cm}^{-1}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$). In SI: Decapoise or $\text{Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ ($\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^{-1}\cdot\text{s}^{-1}$). Note: $1\text{ decapoise} = 10\text{ poise}$.
9. What is critical velocity? ▼
Critical velocity is the threshold velocity of fluid flow up to which motion remains streamline and beyond which it turns turbulent.
10. What do you mean by viscous force? ▼
Viscous force is the internal fluid friction acting tangentially between relative moving layers of fluid.
